A short, opinionated list of works that frame most of what I write about here. Each deserves a post of its own; for now, one line each.

| Year | Work | Why it matters |
|------|------|----------------|
| 1950 | Alan Turing, "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" | Replaces "Can machines think?" with a test — and anticipates most objections. |
| 1972 | Hubert Dreyfus, *What Computers Can't Do* | The phenomenological case against symbolic AI. |
| 1980 | John Searle, "Minds, Brains, and Programs" | The Chinese Room: syntax is not sufficient for semantics. |
| 1985 | John Haugeland, *Artificial Intelligence: The Very Idea* | The clearest account of what "GOFAI" was committed to. |
| 1998 | Andy Clark & David Chalmers, "The Extended Mind" | Where does a mind end, and could a tool be part of one? |
| 2020 | Emily Bender & Alexander Koller, "Climbing towards NLU" | Form versus meaning, argued for the age of language models. |

## How to read them

Start with Turing and Searle together; they are still arguing with each other. Read Dreyfus and Haugeland as a pair on the *old* AI, then ask which of their claims survive the move to learned systems.
